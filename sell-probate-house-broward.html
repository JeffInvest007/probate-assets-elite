How probate works in Broward County

Broward probate matters are heard in the Probate Division of the Seventeenth Judicial Circuit, in the courthouse in downtown Fort Lauderdale.

Where Broward probate is filed

Broward County Courthouse, Probate Division
201 SE 6th Street, Third Floor, Room 03150
Fort Lauderdale, Florida 33301

Probate Division line: (954) 831-7154. Hours are generally Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 3:30 p.m., excluding court holidays. Note that Broward's counter closes earlier than Miami-Dade's, so check before you drive over.

Florida offers more than one route. Formal administration is the full process. Summary administration is the shorter path for smaller estates. Disposition without administration covers a narrow set of very small estates.

Your attorney decides which fits. For selling the house, the practical question is narrower: can the personal representative convey clear title, and will a title company insure it.

The 2026 change that helps a lot of Broward families

On July 1, 2026, Florida raised the value limit for summary administration under Florida Statute 735.201 from $75,000 to $150,000. Homestead property does not count toward that limit.

That is a meaningful shift in a county where a modest house in Margate or North Lauderdale can be most of what an estate holds. Estates that were told last year they had to go the long way may qualify for the shorter path now. Ask your attorney to look again.

You can often start before probate is finished

Estate property in Florida can frequently go under contract while the case is still open, with closing timed to when the personal representative can actually convey title. We coordinate that with the estate's attorney so nothing gets ahead of the court.

Questions Broward families ask

Can you buy a house that is still in probate?+
In many cases yes. The sale can often be assembled while the case is open, with closing timed to when the personal representative can convey clear title. We coordinate with the estate's attorney and a licensed title company. Confirm your own situation with your attorney, because every estate differs.
The house has code violations and an unpaid water bill. Is that a problem?+
Not usually. Municipal code liens, unpaid utilities, property taxes and mortgage balances show up in the title search and get settled from the proceeds at closing. Several Broward cities are aggressive about code fines on vacant homes, which is one more reason not to let the house sit.
How fast can you close?+
Once the estate can convey clear title, we can often close in days rather than months, because we buy for our own account with funds already in place. If your family needs longer, we move at your pace. There is never a clock on our offers.
Do I need my own attorney?+
We encourage it, sincerely. We are not a law firm and we do not give legal advice. An independent attorney reviewing the paperwork is good for you and good for us, because we want every family fully informed before anyone signs.
