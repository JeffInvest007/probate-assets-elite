How probate works in Palm Beach County

Palm Beach County probate runs through the Fifteenth Judicial Circuit. Unlike Miami-Dade and Broward, Palm Beach spreads its offices across the county, which helps if the property is at one end and you are at the other.

Florida offers formal administration for larger estates, summary administration for smaller ones, and disposition without administration in a narrow set of cases. Which applies is your attorney's call. For the house, the practical question is whether the personal representative can convey clear title and whether a title company will insure it.

The 2026 change worth asking your attorney about

On July 1, 2026, Florida raised the summary administration value limit under Florida Statute 735.201 from $75,000 to $150,000. Homestead property does not count toward that limit.

For Palm Beach County families this can matter more than it first appears, because a modest condo plus a bank account is exactly the shape of estate that used to land just over the old line. If you were told last year that the estate had to go the long route, that answer may be different now.

Inherited a condo? A few things to know

Condos are a large share of what changes hands in this county, and they come with wrinkles a single family house does not have.

Association dues keep running. Monthly maintenance does not pause because the owner died. Unpaid dues accrue, and the association can move toward a lien. This is the single most common reason a Palm Beach estate loses money by waiting.

Special assessments. Since Florida tightened structural inspection and reserve requirements for older buildings, a number of associations have levied significant assessments. If one has hit the building, it affects value, and we would rather find it early and be straight with you about it than surprise you later.

Association approval. Many buildings require the association to approve a buyer. We are used to that step and we build it into the timeline instead of pretending it does not exist.

Questions Palm Beach County families ask

I live out of state. Do I have to come to Florida?+
Usually not. Most of it runs remotely. Documents are commonly signed with a remote or mobile notary, and closing is handled by a licensed title company that disburses to heirs wherever they live. Plenty of the families we work with never make the trip.
The condo owes back association dues. Does that kill the deal?+
No, and it is very common. Past due dues, assessments and any association lien get identified in the title search and settled from the proceeds at closing. You do not need to bring those current out of pocket before talking to us.
Can you buy while the estate is still in probate?+
In many cases yes. The sale can often be assembled while the case is open, with closing timed to when the personal representative can convey clear title. We coordinate with the estate's attorney. Confirm your own situation with your attorney, because every estate differs.
Do I need my own attorney?+
We encourage it, sincerely. We are not a law firm and we do not give legal advice. An independent attorney reviewing the paperwork is good for you and good for us, because we want every family fully informed before anyone signs.
